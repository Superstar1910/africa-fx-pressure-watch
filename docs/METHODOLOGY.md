# Methodology

## Analytical question

Is a currency move mainly a temporary liquidity mismatch, a structural external-account deterioration, a weakening policy buffer, a positioning shock, or some combination?

## Five modules

1. **Outcome:** spot rate, change over relevant windows, parallel/interbank spread where lawful and observable, volatility.
2. **USD demand:** energy and other imports, debt service, dividend/repatriation calendars, forward demand.
3. **USD supply:** exports, remittances, tourism, FDI, portfolio flows and official financing.
4. **Buffer and policy:** gross and usable reserves, import cover, intervention, rates and liquidity measures.
5. **Structure:** current account, merchandise balance, external debt and financing quality.

## Classifications

- `supportive`: evidence points toward appreciation or reduced depreciation pressure.
- `balanced`: material signals offset one another.
- `elevated`: depreciation pressure is material but not self-reinforcing.
- `severe`: multiple channels reinforce depreciation and buffers are weakening.
- `unverified`: plausible channel without adequate current evidence.

## Confidence

- **High:** current primary-source observation or directly reproducible calculation.
- **Medium:** credible secondary evidence, lagged primary data, or modest inference.
- **Low:** incomplete, anecdotal, non-comparable or materially stale evidence.

## Score discipline

Scores are configurable summaries used to prioritise investigation. They are not probabilities, price targets or forecasts. Each country must document thresholds appropriate to its exchange-rate regime; a managed peg cannot be interpreted with the same thresholds as a floating currency.

## Publication rule

A country changes from `planned` to `active` only when it has a complete country JSON, at least one primary source for outcome, flows and reserves, explicit cut-off dates, and reviewer sign-off.
